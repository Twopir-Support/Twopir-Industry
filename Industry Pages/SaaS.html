Where Revenue Leaks

Where growth breaks inside a SaaS business

SaaS revenue is not won once. It is re-won every renewal, and it grows only through expansion. That makes every disconnect between product, billing and CRM a recurring cost rather than a one-off inconvenience. What breaks compounds monthly.

🔌

Product usage never reaches the CRM

Activation events, feature adoption and seat consumption sit in the product database and the analytics tool. The account record a rep opens before a call shows none of it.

🧭

Pipeline is built on demo requests, not product signals

Self-serve accounts that are already using the product are treated the same as a cold form fill, so the highest-intent expansion opportunities in the base go unworked.

🧾

Subscription changes live in the billing system alone

Upgrades, downgrades, seat changes and mid-term amendments are recorded in Stripe or Zuora and never written back, so the CRM's view of what a customer actually pays for drifts.

⏳

Renewals are discovered late

Without renewal dates, health signals and payment status on one record, the first sign of a problem is a cancellation notice rather than a declining usage trend two quarters earlier.

📈

Expansion is unowned

Nobody is accountable for the revenue already inside the customer base. Cross-sell and upsell depend on whichever CSM happens to notice, because no system raises the signal.

📊

ARR reporting is assembled by hand

Finance exports billing, RevOps exports the CRM, and the two are reconciled in a spreadsheet each month. Board numbers arrive late, and nobody can drill into why they moved.

Platform Fit

Which system should own which part of the stack

The most expensive architecture mistake in SaaS is asking one platform to be the system of record for something it was never designed to hold. This is how we draw the boundaries — what each platform natively owns, what we build around it, and when it belongs at the centre.

Revenue stack ownership by platform
Platform What it natively owns What Twopir Consulting builds around it Put it at the centre when…
Salesforce Accounts, contacts, opportunities, forecasting, service cases, and a customisable data model with automation and reporting on top. The subscription and lifecycle objects, product-signal ingestion, renewal and expansion motions, and the reporting layer that ties them to billing. You sell to mid-market or enterprise, run a renewal motion, and need one model to survive new products and pricing.
HubSpot Marketing automation, content and campaign management, and a CRM with deal, ticket and quote objects. Salesforce alignment so marketing engagement lands on the same account record, and clean lifecycle-stage definitions across both systems. Growth is marketing-led and self-serve, the deal motion is simple, and speed to launch matters more than model depth.
Stripe Payment processing, subscription plans, invoicing, dunning and the billing events that follow from them. The sync that writes subscription state, payment status and MRR events back onto the account, plus provisioning triggers on those events. Pricing is plan- or usage-based, self-serve checkout matters, and billing complexity is moderate.
Zuora Order-to-revenue for complex subscriptions — amendments, co-terming, proration, revenue recognition and usage rating. The CPQ-to-subscription boundary in Salesforce, amendment workflows, and reporting that reconciles bookings to billed revenue. Contracts are negotiated, amendments are frequent, or revenue recognition is an audit requirement.
Segment / Amplitude Product event capture, identity resolution, behavioural analysis and cohort reporting. The account-level roll-up and scoring model that turns raw events into a signal a rep or CSM can act on inside the CRM. Never at the centre. These are signal sources; making one the revenue system of record is the mistake this table exists to prevent.
NetSuite General ledger, revenue recognition, consolidation, procurement and statutory reporting. The finance boundary — how bookings, subscriptions and invoices map to the GL without finance re-keying anything. Never at the centre for go-to-market. It is the system of record for the books, not for the customer.

The build-in-house comparison. Most SaaS engineering teams can build any one of these connections. The cost is not the first build — it is owning the amendment logic, the identity edge cases and the reconciliation for the next four years, with a roadmap that always has something more valuable on it. We take that ownership on so the product team does not carry revenue plumbing.

Security & Compliance

Built to pass your customers' security review, not just your own

A SaaS company's revenue systems hold its customers' data, which means the architecture is in scope for the same reviews the product is. We design the integration and access model around that from the first session rather than retrofitting it before an audit.

  • SOC 2 evidence — least-privilege integration users, logged access and change control your auditor can trace.
  • GDPR & data subject requests — deletion and export that reach every connected system, not only the CRM.
  • Data residency — integration and storage design that respects regional processing commitments.
  • PII minimisation — product events synced at the level the revenue motion needs, not the level the source emits.
  • Environment discipline — sandbox, staging and production separation with a real release path.
  • Access governance — permission model reviewed against role, not inherited from whoever set the org up.
