The Real Problem

Law Firms Are Running on Manual Processes. Clients Have Moved On.

Legal operations automation isn't a nice-to-have anymore — it's the gap between firms that scale profitably and firms that scale by hiring their way out of every bottleneck. Here's what the data says about where the pressure is coming from.

⏱️

Attorneys spend most of the day on administrative work

The average lawyer captures only a fraction of an eight-hour day as billable work, with the rest lost to intake, document management, scheduling, and reconstructing time entries from memory.

Source: Clio Legal Trends Report
📱

Clients expect faster responses than ever

Legal consumers now expect a same-day reply as the baseline, and firms that respond within minutes rather than hours are dramatically more likely to convert the lead and win the client's trust.

Source: Legal client experience research, 2026
📄

AI meaningfully reduces document preparation time

A substantial share of billable legal tasks — drafting, summarizing, reviewing, and structuring routine documents — can now be assisted or automated with AI, freeing attorneys to focus on judgment and strategy.

Source: Clio Legal Trends Report
📊

Firms are shifting from billable-hour to productivity-based value

As AI compresses the time routine work takes, firms are moving toward flat-fee and value-based pricing models, which reward throughput and client outcomes over hours logged.

Source: Clio Legal Trends Report

AI Case Summary

AI legal workflow

Before every hearing, deposition, or client call, attorneys lose valuable prep time re-reading email threads and court filings to reconstruct where a matter stands. AI keeps that briefing ready in advance.

AI Automatically
  • Summarizes long email threads into key decisions and open items
  • Summarizes court documents and filings into plain-language briefs
  • Prepares an attorney briefing ahead of hearings and client calls
  • Identifies upcoming deadlines and statute-of-limitations risks
  • Extracts key facts, parties, and dates from case files
